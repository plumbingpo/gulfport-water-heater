Keeping Your Ocean Springs Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Most water heater failures we see in Ocean Springs start with what's in the water. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod is often used up, leaving the steel open to rust. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: thermocouples, burners, and venting on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Code requirements add another layer. Swapping out an older heater frequently means bringing the install up to current code: a thermal expansion tank if the water system is closed, a T&P discharge line that terminates correctly, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, plus straps and a pan where required. Well water brings its own challenges, such as extra sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that sometimes shows up when the water reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most are much cheaper to address early.
